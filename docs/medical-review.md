## 1. Emergency numbers and danger signs (SOS screen)

- **112**: National emergency (Police, fire or ambulance)
- **193**: Ambulance (National Ambulance Service)
- **191**: Police (Ghana Police Service)
- **192**: Fire (Ghana National Fire Service)

Shown as "Call for help now if you see":

- Chest pain or pressure, especially spreading to the arm, jaw or back
- Struggling to breathe, or lips turning blue
- Face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech (possible stroke)
- Fainting, a seizure, or being very hard to wake
- Heavy bleeding that will not stop
- Fever with a stiff neck, confusion or a rash that does not fade when pressed
- A baby under 3 months with a fever
- Severe pain in the belly, or vomiting blood
- Thoughts of ending your life

## 2. Symptoms Checker rules

Step 1 asks about these general danger signs. Any one leads straight to "Get emergency care now":

- Severe difficulty breathing
- Chest pain or pressure
- Fainting, a fit (seizure) or very hard to wake
- Confusion or slurred speech
- Heavy bleeding that will not stop
- Face drooping or sudden weakness on one side
- Vomiting everything, or unable to drink
- Thoughts of ending your life

Then: who is unwell (adult, child under 5, pregnant, over 65) → main problem → follow-up questions → how long → how bad.

Follow-up questions (🔴 = emergency, 🟠 = see a health worker today):

**Fever or feeling hot**
- 🔴 Stiff neck, or a rash that does not fade when pressed?
- 🟠 Yellow eyes or very dark urine?
- 🟠 Temperature 39.5°C or higher?

**Headache**
- 🔴 Sudden, severe "worst ever" headache?
- 🔴 Blurred vision, or a headache in late pregnancy?
- 🟠 Also a fever?

**Cough or breathing**
- 🔴 Breathing fast, or the chest pulls in with each breath?
- 🟠 Coughing up blood?
- 🟠 Cough for more than 2 weeks, or night sweats and weight loss?

**Diarrhoea or vomiting**
- 🔴 Very thirsty, sunken eyes, or little or no urine?
- 🟠 Blood in the stool?
- 🔴 Lots of watery "rice-water" stool?

**Belly pain**
- 🔴 Severe pain, or a hard, swollen belly?
- 🔴 Pregnant or might be, with pain or bleeding?
- 🟠 Also a fever?

**Skin rash**
- 🔴 Swelling of the lips, face or tongue?
- 🔴 Blisters, peeling skin, or sores in the mouth or eyes?
- 🟠 Red, hot, spreading skin, or a fever?

**Pain passing urine**
- 🟠 Pain in the side or back, with fever or shivering?
- 🟠 Blood in the urine?
- 🟠 Pregnant?

**Something else**
- 🟠 Getting worse quickly?

Other rules: any fever → malaria test advice and "see a health worker today" (also for fever in child under 5 / pregnancy / over 65). Diarrhoea in a child under 5, or belly pain or headache in pregnancy → today. Severe → today. More than a week → book a visit. 4–7 days and not mild → book a visit. Moderate → pharmacist.

### Example results

**Adult, headache, today, mild** → Care for yourself at home

- Drink water and rest in a quiet, dark room.
- Paracetamol can help. Avoid taking painkillers every day.
- Check your blood pressure if you can.
- Go to hospital at once if you notice any danger sign: trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, confusion, a fit, heavy bleeding or being unable to drink.

**Adult, fever, 1–3 days, moderate** → See a health worker today

- Get a malaria test (RDT). Pharmacies, chemical shops and clinics can test you in 15 minutes. Do not take malaria medicine without a test.
- Drink plenty of clean water.
- Paracetamol can bring the fever down. Follow the dose on the pack.
- Rest, and wear light clothing.
- Go to hospital at once if you notice any danger sign: trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, confusion, a fit, heavy bleeding or being unable to drink.

**Child under 5, diarrhoea, today, mild** → See a health worker today

- Drink ORS (oral rehydration salts) after every loose stool.
- Keep eating light foods such as rice water or koko.
- Children: give zinc tablets for 10 to 14 days if available.
- Wash hands with soap after the toilet.
- Go to hospital at once if you notice any danger sign: trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, confusion, a fit, heavy bleeding or being unable to drink.

**Adult, cough, more than a week, mild** → Book a clinic visit

- Drink warm fluids. Honey in warm water soothes a cough (not for babies under 1).
- Rest and avoid smoke.
- Sleep propped up if breathing is uncomfortable.
- Go to hospital at once if you notice any danger sign: trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, confusion, a fit, heavy bleeding or being unable to drink.

**Adult, pain passing urine, 1–3 days, moderate** → Talk to a pharmacist

- Drink plenty of water.
- Do not hold urine for long.
- Urine infections usually need antibiotics prescribed by a health worker.
- Go to hospital at once if you notice any danger sign: trouble breathing, chest pain, fainting, confusion, a fit, heavy bleeding or being unable to drink.

Accepted ranges (to catch typos): Blood pressure 60–260 mmHg; Blood sugar 1–35 mmol/L; Weight 2–300 kg; Temperature 33–43 °C.

## 5. Health Library articles

### Malaria: prevent, test, treat
_Every fever in Ghana should be tested for malaria. Here is how to stay protected._ (Source: Ghana Health Service National Malaria Elimination Programme; WHO)

Malaria is spread by mosquitoes that bite mostly between dusk and dawn. It is common all year in Ghana and is most dangerous for young children and pregnant women.

**Prevent**
- Sleep under a treated mosquito net every night, even in the dry season.
- Clear standing water around the house: old tins, tyres and blocked gutters.
- Pregnant women should attend antenatal care, where they receive medicine (SP) to prevent malaria.

**Test every fever**
- A fever, chills, headache, body pains or vomiting can be malaria. Get a rapid diagnostic test (RDT) at a pharmacy, chemical shop or clinic. It takes about 15 minutes.
- Do not take malaria medicine without a positive test. Many fevers have other causes, such as typhoid or infections that need different treatment.

**Treat fully**
- If the test is positive, take the full course of the medicine you are given (usually an ACT such as artemether-lumefantrine), even when you feel better.
- Go to hospital at once for confusion, fits, very yellow eyes, dark urine, trouble breathing or being unable to drink.

### Understanding high blood pressure
_It often has no symptoms, but it is a leading cause of stroke. Know your numbers._ (Source: WHO hypertension fact sheet; Ghana Ministry of Health)

- Blood pressure is written as two numbers, like 120/80. The top number is the pressure when the heart beats; the bottom is when it rests.
- A reading of 140/90 or higher on several days means high blood pressure (hypertension). Many people in Ghana have it without knowing.

**Why it matters**
Over time, high blood pressure damages the heart, kidneys, eyes and brain. It is the main cause of strokes.

**What helps**
- Use less salt and fewer stock cubes; flavour food with fresh herbs, ginger and garlic instead.
- Walk briskly for 30 minutes on most days.
- Eat more vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains.
- Limit alcohol and do not smoke.
- If you are given medicine, take it every day, even when you feel well. Do not stop without talking to your health worker.

**Get help now if**
Your reading is 180/120 or higher, especially with chest pain, a severe headache, weakness on one side or trouble speaking. Call 112.

### Living well with diabetes
_Everyday habits that keep blood sugar steady and prevent complications._ (Source: WHO diabetes fact sheet; International Diabetes Federation)

Diabetes means the body cannot control blood sugar properly. Common signs are being very thirsty, passing urine often, tiredness, blurred vision and slow-healing wounds.

**Food**
- Fill half the plate with vegetables, a quarter with protein (fish, beans, eggs, chicken) and a quarter with starch.
- Choose smaller portions of fufu, banku, kenkey and rice, and prefer unripe plantain, brown rice and beans.
- Cut down on sugary drinks, malt drinks and sweet bread.

**Everyday care**
- Take your medicine as prescribed and check your sugar if you have a meter.
- Check your feet every day for cuts or sores, and never walk barefoot.
- Have your eyes, kidneys and blood pressure checked regularly.

**Low sugar (hypo)**
Shaking, sweating, confusion or feeling faint can mean low sugar. Take something sweet at once, such as juice or sweets, then eat a meal.

### Staying hydrated in the heat
_How much to drink, the warning signs of dehydration, and how to use ORS._ (Source: WHO; Ghana Health Service)

Ghana’s heat, especially during the harmattan and in the north, makes the body lose water quickly. Most adults need about 2 to 3 litres a day, more when working outside.

**Warning signs**
Dark urine, headache, dizziness, a dry mouth and tiredness are early signs. Confusion or fainting needs urgent care.

**Tips**
- Drink clean water regularly, not only when thirsty.
- Rest in the shade during the hottest hours.
- For diarrhoea or heavy sweating, use ORS (oral rehydration salts) mixed exactly as the packet says.
- Babies under 6 months only need breast milk, even in hot weather.

### Sickle cell: what to know
_Know your genotype, and how to prevent painful crises._ (Source: Sickle Cell Foundation of Ghana; WHO)

- Sickle cell disease is inherited. People with SS or SC genotypes have the disease; people with AS carry the trait and are usually well.
- Two AS partners have a 1 in 4 chance with each pregnancy of a child with sickle cell disease. Knowing your genotype helps you plan.

**Preventing crises**
- Drink plenty of water every day.
- Avoid getting too cold or too hot, and rest when tired.
- Sleep under a mosquito net. Malaria can trigger a crisis.
- Take folic acid and any preventive medicine your clinic gives you, and keep up with vaccines.

**Get help now if**
There is severe pain that will not ease, a fever, chest pain, trouble breathing, a swollen belly, weakness on one side, or a painful erection that lasts more than 2 hours.

### A healthy pregnancy
_Antenatal visits, warning signs, and what to eat._ (Source: Ghana Health Service Maternal Health Programme; WHO)

Start antenatal care as early as possible and attend at least 8 visits. Antenatal care is free under the NHIS.

**Look after yourself**
- Take the iron and folic acid tablets you are given.
- Sleep under a treated mosquito net.
- Eat a varied diet with beans, fish, eggs, leafy greens such as kontomire, and fruit.
- Avoid alcohol, smoking and medicines not prescribed for you.

**Go to hospital at once for**
Bleeding from the vagina, a severe headache or blurred vision, swelling of the face or hands, fits, fever, severe belly pain, or the baby moving less than usual.

### Danger signs in young children
_When a sick child needs a health worker immediately._ (Source: WHO Integrated Management of Childhood Illness (IMCI))

Young children can become very sick quickly. Take a child to a health facility at once if they have any of these:

- Unable to drink or breastfeed.
- Vomiting everything.
- Fits (convulsions).
- Very sleepy or hard to wake.
- Fast breathing, or the chest pulling in with each breath.
- Fever in a baby under 2 months.

**At home**
- Keep breastfeeding and giving fluids during any illness.
- For diarrhoea, give ORS and zinc.
- Keep up with vaccines on the Child Health Record book (the "weighing card").

### Looking after your mind
_Stress and low mood are common. Small steps help, and so does talking._ (Source: Mental Health Authority Ghana; WHO)

Everyone feels stressed or sad sometimes. When low mood, worry or poor sleep lasts more than two weeks and makes daily life hard, it is worth getting support.

**Small steps**
- Talk to someone you trust: a friend, family member, faith leader or health worker.
- Keep a routine: regular sleep, meals and some movement every day.
- Spend time outside and with people you enjoy.
- Limit alcohol, which makes low mood worse.

**Get help**
- Mental health care is available at many hospitals and through the Mental Health Authority.
- If you are thinking of ending your life or are in danger, call 112 now or go to the nearest hospital. You deserve support.

### First aid: cuts and burns
_What to do in the first minutes, and what not to put on a burn._ (Source: Ghana Red Cross Society; WHO)

**Cuts**
- Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth for 10 minutes to stop bleeding.
- Rinse with clean water and cover with a clean dressing.
- Get help for deep cuts, cuts that will not stop bleeding, or animal bites. Ask about a tetanus injection.

**Burns**
- Cool the burn under cool running water for 20 minutes.
- Remove rings or tight clothing nearby, but not anything stuck to the skin.
- Cover loosely with cling film or a clean cloth.
- Do not put toothpaste, oil, eggs or butter on a burn.
- Go to hospital for large, deep or facial burns, and for burns on children.

### Typhoid and safe water
_How typhoid spreads and simple ways to keep food and water safe._ (Source: WHO typhoid fact sheet)

Typhoid spreads through food and water contaminated with sewage. It causes a long fever, stomach pain, headache and weakness, and needs testing and antibiotics from a health worker.

**Prevent**
- Drink treated, boiled or sealed water.
- Wash hands with soap after the toilet and before eating or cooking.
- Eat food that is freshly cooked and hot. Wash fruit and vegetables in clean water.
- A typhoid vaccine is available at many clinics.

## 6. Healthy Recipes

### Light kontomire stew (Pregnancy, Low salt, High fibre)
Cocoyam leaves with egg and a little palm oil. Rich in iron and folate.

**Ingredients:** 2 large bunches kontomire (cocoyam leaves), washed and chopped; 2 tablespoons palm oil; 1 onion, chopped; 3 tomatoes, blended; 1 thumb ginger and 2 cloves garlic, grated; 2 tablespoons ground agushie (melon seeds); 4 boiled eggs or 1 small tin mackerel; Fresh pepper to taste, and a small pinch of salt

**Tip:** Skip the stock cube; the ginger, garlic and agushie give plenty of flavour.

### Red-red with less oil (High fibre, Vegetarian, Diabetes friendly)
Black-eyed bean stew with boiled or baked plantain instead of fried.

**Ingredients:** 2 cups black-eyed beans, soaked overnight; 2 tablespoons palm oil; 1 onion, chopped; 2 tomatoes, chopped; 1 teaspoon ground ginger or a thumb of fresh ginger; 2 ripe-ish plantains; Pepper and a small pinch of salt

**Tip:** Firmer, less ripe plantain raises blood sugar more slowly.

### Grilled tilapia with pepper sauce (Low salt, Diabetes friendly)
A lean, protein-rich dinner with a fresh tomato and onion relish.

**Ingredients:** 2 medium tilapia, cleaned and scored; 1 thumb ginger, 3 cloves garlic, 1 small onion, blended; Juice of 1 lime; 2 tomatoes, 1 onion and 1 green pepper, finely chopped; Fresh chilli to taste

**Tip:** Fill half your plate with the vegetable relish and keep the banku portion to the size of your fist.

### Hausa koko with groundnuts (Quick, Vegetarian, High fibre)
A warming millet porridge breakfast, lightly sweetened.

**Ingredients:** 1 cup millet koko paste; 3 cups water; Ginger, cloves and grains of selim (hwentia) to taste; 1 teaspoon sugar or honey per bowl; A handful of roasted groundnuts; Milk (optional)

**Tip:** The groundnuts add protein that keeps you full for longer than koge or sugar bread.

### Garden egg and avocado salad (Quick, Vegetarian, Diabetes friendly, High fibre)
A no-cook lunch: crunchy garden eggs, avocado, beans and lime.

**Ingredients:** 4 garden eggs, sliced; 1 avocado, cubed; 1 cup cooked beans; 1 tomato and ½ onion, chopped; Juice of 1 lime, a little oil, pepper

**Tip:** Garden eggs are low in calories and high in fibre, which is great for steady blood sugar.

### Chicken light soup (Low salt, Pregnancy)
A clear, spicy soup that comforts when you are unwell.

**Ingredients:** 1 kg chicken pieces, skin removed; 1 onion, 3 tomatoes, 2 garden eggs; 1 thumb ginger, 3 cloves garlic; Fresh pepper to taste; 6 cups water and a small pinch of salt

**Tip:** Removing the chicken skin cuts most of the fat without losing flavour.
